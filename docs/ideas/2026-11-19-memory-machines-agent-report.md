**Method.** About 35 web searches, plus reading each primary source (OCR saved in `/projects/sandbox/_scratch/hist2/`). "No implementation found" means none turned up in these searches, not proof.

### 1. Animal Spirits: Descartes's hydraulic memory (recommended)

- **Source:** Descartes, *L'Homme* (written early 1630s, published 1662/1664), articles 72–73 in Clerselier's numbering. 1677 French edition: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/lhommeetlaformat00desc). English of art. 72: [UVA Metaphors](https://metaphors.iath.virginia.edu/metaphors/19561).
- **Proposed:** Spirits from the pineal gland H flow through tubes into the pores between the fibres of brain part B, widen them and trace figures there. Stronger, longer or repeated flow makes the figures last, so the idea can re-form on the gland without the object. In art. 73, re-opening some holes (a, b) re-opens others (c, d) that were often opened with them. His example: seeing two eyes and a nose, he at once imagines the forehead and mouth. **Never done:** Descartes described a hypothetical machine. I found no build.
- **Build:** a flow network (gland surface → tubes → part-B pore lattice) solved every frame. Pore width grows with the flow through it and slowly relaxes. Recall is gland pressure with the sensory valves shut. The remembered figure is the gland's outflow pattern.
- **Demo:** Show your face to the webcam, then cover your mouth and forehead. Spirits run through the pores your face widened, and the missing parts flow back onto the gland. This is Descartes's own example, running.
- **Prior art:** Five queries, for example "Descartes Treatise of Man memory pores animal spirits computational simulation", "Descartes hydraulic brain model simulation pineal gland animal spirits interactive" and "Descartes pineal gland animal spirits interactive simulation WebGL OR github"; a "working model OR reconstruction" query had no hits. I found only scholarship, for example Sutton reading it as a distributed memory model ([PDF](https://philarchive.org/go.pl?id=SUTDMC&proxyId=&u=https://philpapers.org/archive/SUTDMC.pdf)). The closest mechanism is slime-mould tube networks that store a food location in tube diameters ([Kramar & Alim 2021](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2007815118); [memory capacity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11192)). That is a different design and stores no images. **No working implementation found.**
- **Feasibility and risk:** Runs in the browser on WebGPU or CPU. A 32–64 px gland means 10⁴–10⁵ edges and one sparse solve per frame. Main risk: Descartes leaves part B's geometry and the re-opening rule vague. We would add a pressure-threshold pore rule, labelled as ours. Capacity may be only a few faces, and recall may blur. It could read as "Hopfield in pipes" (like the rejected Attractor); showing the flow answers that. Kill-test it on day 1 with a 16×16 gland.
- **Verdict:** Best on all five items: an unbuilt 1630s memory machine whose demo is in the source text, with spirits and a seat of the soul for Halloween.

### 2. The Radiating Soul: Hooke's 1682 memory model

- **Source:** Hooke's 1682 Royal Society lecture, printed in *Posthumous Works* (1705), "Lectures of Light", from p. 140: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/b30454621_0001). [Hintzman 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12747488/) judges it more complete than Semon's theory.
- **Proposed:** The soul at the centre forms material ideas and pushes older ones outward into a growing sphere. It senses them by radiating like the sun. Their reaction falls with the square of distance, so old memories are faint, and distance tells how long ago. Ideas lying in between can block the radiation like an eclipse, so a memory seems lost until the obstacle moves. Similar ideas reinforce each other like strings in unison, so a new idea makes distant similar ones notable ("This puts me in mind"). Recall renews an idea and forms a fresh copy at the centre. **Never done:** I found no build.
- **Build:** a 3D sphere of webcam-snapshot ideas, placed and crowded by Hooke's rules; the soul ray-casts with inverse-square falloff and occlusion; resonance uses simple image features.
- **Demo:** Show a skull, then ten other objects, then the skull again. Far out in the dark the old skull glows in sympathy, the beam swings to it, and the machine says how long ago it saw it.
- **Prior art:** Queries: "Hintzman 2003 'Robert Hooke's model of memory' … simulation"; "Hooke 1682 memory model implemented simulation repository ideas soul radiation inverse square recency"; "'Hooke' 'model of memory' computational OR simulation OR interactive OR visualization -Hintzman". I found Hintzman's analysis (with a forgetting equation, not a running system) and historical chapters ([2020](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-39375-5_5)). **No working implementation found.**
- **Feasibility and risk:** Easy (three.js or WebGPU, hundreds of ideas). Hooke never defines "similar motions", so the similarity measure is ours. It may look like the rejected memory palace. Keep resonance a recall cue, not a sound store, to stay clear of the holophone.
- **Verdict:** Strong runner-up: the most mechanistic pre-modern memory theory, unbuilt, with three demoable effects.

### 3. The Wish Machine: Freud's 1895 *Project*

- **Source:** Freud, "Project for a Scientific Psychology" (1895, published 1950). English translation: [hosted PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/9/19778/files/2021/12/freud-project_for_scientific_psychology.pdf).
- **Proposed:** φ, ψ and ω neurones pass quantity. Memory is the *differences* in facilitation of ψ contact-barriers (Part I §3). After an experience of satisfaction, renewed urgency activates the object's memory-image and first produces a hallucination (§11). Inhibition by a cathected ego lets an "indication of reality" from ω separate perception from memory (§15). **Abandoned:** the editor notes the part on repression was never completed. Within weeks Freud wrote that he no longer understood the state of mind that produced it.
- **Build:** a few hundred neurones with quantity flow, facilitation, rising internal urgency, an inhibiting ego group and ω discharge reports. Low-resolution webcam frames are its φ input. The user is the helper who feeds it.
- **Demo:** Feed it while showing a cup. Let urgency build: a ghost cup appears on its perception screen, it acts, and nothing satisfies it. Switch on the ego: the ghost dims, and only the real cup fires the indication of reality.
- **Prior art:** Six queries, for example "Freud 1895 Project phi psi omega neurons implemented simulation contact barriers facilitation computer model", "simulation of Freud's Project experience of satisfaction wishful hallucination neural network", and German "Entwurf einer Psychologie Simulation neuronales Netz". I found historical analyses ([JNNP 2011](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/82/2/122)) and TU Wien's SiMA/ARS agents built on psychoanalytic metapsychology ([Lang 2010](https://sima.ict.tuwien.ac.at/new/wp-content/uploads/2012/06/Diss-Lang.pdf)). As far as I could tell, they do not implement the 1895 neurone model. **No working implementation of it found.**
- **Feasibility and risk:** Trivial compute. The text gives no numbers, so most parameters are interpretation, and the result can look like a toy reinforcement-learning agent.
- **Verdict:** Best story and spookiest; more interpretation and adjacent prior art put it third.

### Dropped or downgraded

- **Semon's Mneme (1904):** no implementation found, and it is literally "the actual engram". But Semon states laws, not a mechanism. He called physical storage "hardly ripe yet" for discussion (*The Mneme* p. 116, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/cu31924100387210)), so a build reduces to cue-triggered replay.
- **J. Z. Young's mnemon (1965):** older simulations exist, for example a Michigan "Computer Simulation Model of Attack-learning Behavior in the Octopus" ([record](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/b1332231-0e25-4d49-9982-cf5a95308e7b)). Not checked in detail.
- **Bent Russell's 1913 hydraulic learning machine:** known through [Cordeschi](https://philpapers.org/rec/COREM-4). I did not find the primary text or confirm whether it was built, and it overlaps #1.
- **Ashby's DAMS** (little public detail found) and **Herbart's 1824 mechanics of ideas** ([reanalysed in 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11623920/)): weak 30-second demos.
